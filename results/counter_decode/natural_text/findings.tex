\section{Question}

The F2.1 figure in \texttt{results/counter\_decode/figures/F2.1\_calibration\_gap.png}
shows the streaming Laplace $\hat\beta$ filter drifting substantially during
$\beta$-decoded generation: $\log\hat\beta$ reaches $\approx +1.3$ at
$\beta_{\text{dec}}=2$ and $\approx -3.3$ at $\beta_{\text{dec}}=0.5$ by $t=199$,
growing with $t$ and anti-symmetric in $\log\beta_{\text{dec}}$.
The framework reads this as evidence the model's response to $\beta$-decoded
prefixes is non-multiplicative.
A weaker alternative: the filter has baseline drift even with no temperature
manipulation, and the F2.1 gap is partly the filter's own pathology rather than
a property of the imprint.
This experiment rules out the weaker alternative.

\section{Setup}

Same model (Qwen-2.5-3B), same filter parameters
($\sigma_0 = \sigmaZero$, log-normal Laplace, $\alpha = \gamma = 1$) as F0/F2.
Four conditions, $\nPassagesWikiSelf$ passages each, 200 filter updates per passage:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{WikiText / self\_gen} --- prompt = first 20 tokens of a
WikiText-103 article; the model samples the next 200 tokens at
$\beta_{\text{dec}}=1$; filter sees $(\ell_t, x_t)$ with
$x_t \sim \mathrm{softmax}(\ell_t)$.
\item \textbf{WikiText / natural} --- same 20-token prompt;
the next 200 tokens are the real article continuation.
\item \textbf{WritingPrompts / self\_gen} --- same protocol as WikiText/self\_gen
but with WritingPrompts stories as the prompt source.
\item \textbf{WritingPrompts / natural} --- same as WritingPrompts/self\_gen
but $x_t$ is the real story token.
\end{itemize}

The two \textit{self\_gen} arms are the actual no-imprint null consistent with
F2.1's sampling protocol --- the only way to make $x_t$ i.i.d.\ with respect to
$\mathrm{softmax}(\ell_t)$, which is the distribution under which the filter's
score $\ell_t[x_t] - E_q[\ell_t]$ is mean-zero by construction.
The two \textit{natural} arms test the model's calibration on out-of-distribution
text.

The headline x-axis is cumulative Fisher
$\Sigma_\tau \mathrm{Var}_q(\tau) = J_t - J_0$ (the filter's effective sample size),
not $t$.
Different corpora accumulate $J$ at different rates because of entropy
differences; using $\Sigma\mathrm{Fisher}$ controls for that.
Each condition is shown over its own data range so we don't hide tail behavior
of low-entropy corpora.

Three structural observations.

\paragraph{(a) Self\_gen flatlines on both corpora.}
Median $\log\hat\beta_\infty = \logBetaFinalMedWikiSelf$ (WikiText/self\_gen,
$t = \logBetaFinalTWikiSelf$ vs.\ zero)
and $\logBetaFinalMedWPSelf$ (WritingPrompts/self\_gen, $t = \logBetaFinalTWPSelf$).
Both are within 1 SE of zero.
This says: under self-sampling at $\beta_{\text{dec}}=1$ --- the no-imprint
null --- the filter shows no baseline drift detectable at this sample size.
The data is consistent with the filter being asymptotically unbiased.

\paragraph{(b) WikiText/natural agrees with self\_gen.}
Paired difference WikiText/natural $-$ WikiText/self\_gen $= \pairedMeanDiffWiki$
(mean), SE $= \pairedSeDiffWiki$, $t = \pairedTStatWiki$.
The two arms are statistically indistinguishable on Wikipedia.
Qwen-2.5-3B is well-calibrated there: real Wikipedia next-tokens look exactly
as the model expects under $\mathrm{softmax}(\ell_t)$.

\paragraph{(c) WritingPrompts/natural drifts negative, robustly.}
Median $\log\hat\beta_\infty = \logBetaFinalMedWPNat$ on the natural arm.
Paired against WritingPrompts/self\_gen --- holding the prompt fixed and varying
only the source of $x_t$ --- the difference is $\pairedMeanDiffWP$ (mean),
SE $= \pairedSeDiffWP$, $t = \pairedTStatWP$ ($p < 10^{-3}$).
The trimmed median (dropping 3 outliers per tail) equals
$\logBetaFinalTrimMedWPNat$, identical to the untrimmed median, so the offset
isn't driven by a few catastrophic passages.
Qwen-2.5-3B is over-confident on actual Reddit creative writing: real
WritingPrompts tokens systematically have lower logits than
$\mathrm{softmax}(\ell_t)$ would predict.

\section{What this implies for F2.1}

This experiment establishes one narrow but useful fact:
\emph{the filter has no detectable baseline drift on i.i.d.\ samples from the model's own distribution},
on either corpus.
F2.1 shows $|\log\hat\beta|$ reaching 1.3--3.3 at extreme $\beta_{\text{dec}}$ values,
which is at least an order of magnitude larger than the largest natural-text
offset we measured ($\logBetaFinalMedWPNat$ on WritingPrompts).
So \emph{the F2.1 drift is not filter-side baseline pathology}.

This is strictly narrower than "the F2.1 drift is imprint structure."
The natural-text experiment does not test what the filter does when fed
$\beta$-decoded prefixes that may be non-multiplicatively distorted relative to
the imprint model.
A filter that correctly handles self-sampled data can still misattribute a
non-multiplicative distortion to $\beta$ if the imprint hypothesis is wrong.
So F2.1's drift could reflect (i) a real multiplicative imprint that the filter
recovers correctly, or (ii) a non-multiplicative distortion that the filter
interprets through its imprint prior.
This experiment cannot distinguish (i) from (ii).
It only rules out (iii) baseline filter drift unrelated to the decoder.

\section{A separate finding: out-of-distribution calibration offset}

Independently of the F2.1 question, the natural arm establishes that Qwen-2.5-3B
has a small but real over-confidence offset on Reddit creative writing
($\pairedMeanDiffWP$ in log-$\beta$ space against the self-gen baseline, paired
$t = \pairedTStatWP$) and no detectable offset on Wikipedia
(paired $t = \pairedTStatWiki$).
This is a confound for any future use of the filter on out-of-distribution
corpora.

Whether this offset contaminates the F2.1 result on the existing F0 prompts is
\emph{not measured here}.
The F0 prompts (\texttt{src/decoding\_decoding/prompts.py}) include fiction and
dialogue genres that may inherit the WritingPrompts-like offset.
Verifying this would require running the natural-text filter on held-out natural
continuations of each F0 prompt --- a small follow-up experiment.

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Two corpora is two corpora.}
The headline claim "filter is well-specified under self-sampling" is corpus-independent,
but the magnitude of the WritingPrompts offset reflects that one dataset.

\item \textbf{Trajectory-to-trajectory variance is wide.}
Per-passage IQRs span $\sim 0.4$ in log-$\beta$ space, dominated by the
first few filter steps where $J_t$ is small and individual updates move
$\hat\beta$ a lot.
Median-based statistics are stable, but tail behavior is undersampled at $N=64$.

\item \textbf{The natural-text test doesn't reach the framework's load-bearing claim.}
F2.1 shows non-multiplicative behavior under $\beta$-decoding; this experiment
shows the filter doesn't invent that behavior on natural text.
It does not isolate whether the $\beta$-decoded drift is faithful multiplicative
imprint or some other distortion the filter is reading through its prior.
\end{itemize}

\section{Bottom line}

\textit{What this rules out:}
the F2.1 calibration gap is not artifact of filter-side baseline drift.
On i.i.d.\ samples from $\mathrm{softmax}(\ell_t)$, the filter holds
$\log\hat\beta$ within $\sim 0.07$ of zero (within 1 SE) regardless of corpus.

\textit{What this rules in:}
a small ($\sim 0.3$ log-units) calibration offset on creative-writing text that
should be treated as a confound on any future filter use on out-of-distribution
data.

\textit{What this does not address:}
whether the F2.1 drift is true multiplicative imprint vs.\ a non-multiplicative
distortion the filter is misinterpreting.
That requires a different experiment.
