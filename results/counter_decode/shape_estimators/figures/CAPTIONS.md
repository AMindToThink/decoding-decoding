# Captions for `results/counter_decode/shape_estimators/figures/`

Generated by `scripts/analyze_shape_estimator.py`, reading the sweep written by
`scripts/run_shape_estimator_sweep.py`. Two non-bootstrap, shape-based β̂
estimators are compared against the streaming Laplace baseline at σ_0 = 0.2,
γ = 1.0:
- `rank_gap`: median over K = 20 ranks of Δ_k / Δ_k^(ref), where Δ_k is the
  k-th sorted logit gap at step t and the reference is taken from the last 5
  positions of the un-corrected ("real prompt") logits.
- `renyi_infty`: bisection for β̂_t such that
  H_∞(softmax(ℓ_t / β̂_t)) = H_∞^(ref), where H_∞ = −log max p and the
  reference is the same last-5-position aggregate.
Full context in `../findings.md`.

## shape_traj.png — per-step trajectories, three estimators side-by-side

**Generated by.** `plot_trajectories` in `scripts/analyze_shape_estimator.py`.

**Layout.** Four rows × three columns. Columns: estimator
(rank_gap, renyi_infty, laplace_baseline). Rows top-to-bottom: log β̂_t,
log β_dec_t, entropy of P_sample(t), max P_sample(t). Each panel overlays
five curves, one per β_target ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 4.0} (coolwarm
colormap, blue = 0.25, red = 4.0). Shared y-axis within each row.

**What to read.** A head-to-head visual of the three estimators. Top row: all
three split β̂_t into a clean β_target ordering — log β̂ moves up monotonically
with β_target. The shape-based estimators are visibly *noisier* trajectory by
trajectory (rank_gap and renyi_infty fluctuate ±0.5 nats per step) because
they read β̂_t from a single step's sorted-logit shape, with no streaming
Bayesian smoothing. The Laplace baseline is smooth by construction. Second
row (log β_dec_t): under the rank_gap and renyi_infty estimators the high-β
arms produce β_dec that drifts *up* — they do not self-neutralize as
aggressively as the Laplace, which means the corrector keeps applying
real sharpening/flattening at the high-β end. Rows 3–4 (entropy, max P)
show the downstream sampling consequence: the shape estimators give wider
entropy *separation* across β_target than the Laplace baseline at the high-β
end, especially renyi_infty. The cost is that, per step, the shape-estimator
samples are noisier.

## shape_vs_btarget.png — late stats vs β_target, three estimators

**Generated by.** `plot_late_vs_btarget` in
`scripts/analyze_shape_estimator.py`. "Late" averages the last 30 tokens.

**Layout.** One row × four panels. X-axis: β_target on log scale. Panels:
late log β̂, late log β_dec, late entropy of P_sample, late max P_sample.
Three curves per panel: rank_gap (blue), renyi_infty (orange),
laplace_baseline (green).

**What to read.** The headline summary: **renyi_infty has wider dynamic range
than the Laplace baseline.** All three estimators agree at β_target ∈ {0.25,
0.5} (left ends of every panel are nearly stacked), and they share the same
qualitative monotonicity in β̂. Above β_target = 1, renyi_infty's β_dec rises
to log β_dec ≈ 1.1 (β_dec ≈ 3) at β_target = 4, vs the Laplace's
log β_dec ≈ 0.6 (β_dec ≈ 1.8). That is, the renyi_infty corrector keeps
sharpening the decoder at high β_target instead of self-neutralizing, which
is the saturation failure mode visible in `extreme_sweep/findings.md` for
the Laplace baseline. rank_gap saturates around log β̂ ≈ 0 (β̂ ≈ 1) for
β_target ≥ 2 — its β̂ ceiling is *lower* than the other two, because the
K = 20 ranks all hit the discrete top-token cap at sharp temperatures and the
median ratio stops growing. Right two panels show the consequence for
samples: at β_target = 4, renyi_infty produces a flatter (entropy ≈ 1) and
less-greedy (max P ≈ 0.85) distribution than the Laplace (entropy ≈ 0.4,
max P ≈ 0.88), so the corrector is doing more real work. This is the basis
for the "Rényi-∞ as a non-saturating drop-in" suggestion in
`../findings.md`, including the user's "partial counter-decoding"
proposal β_dec = β_target / β̂^α to dial the strength continuously.
