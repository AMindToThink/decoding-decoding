\section{Question}

The class proposal's headline framing positions the streaming Laplace
$\hat\beta$ filter as ``online temperature scaling [Guo17] converging in the
large-data limit to the same global $T$ Guo's batch MLE fits.''  This
experiment tests that claim directly.

For each of WikiText-103 and WritingPrompts, we (i) fit two
$\hat\beta$ estimators on a calibration half---streaming
($\hat\beta_{\mathrm{stream}}$, per-passage filter, aggregated by median, and
a pooled single-trajectory variant) and Guo MLE
($\hat\beta_{\mathrm{MLE}}$, scipy on the calibration-half NLL), and
(ii) evaluate the per-token test PPL under each.

\section{Setup}

Qwen-2.5-3B, no fine-tuning. \calNcal{} calibration passages plus
\calNtest{} test passages per corpus, each tokenized to exactly
\calLength{} tokens. Passage-level (not token-level) split: passages
0--\calNcal{} are calibration, passages \calNcal{}--$2\,\calNcal$ are test.
Filter: $\sigma_0 = \calSigmaZero$, warmup \calWarmup{} tokens, $\calU$
filter steps per passage.

Calibration estimators:
\begin{description}
\item[$\hat\beta_{\mathrm{stream\_med}}$.] Run the existing Laplace filter
(\texttt{counter\_decode.laplace\_update}) per-passage with state reset
between passages; take the median final $\hat\eta$ across passages.
\item[$\hat\beta_{\mathrm{stream\_pooled}}$.] One filter trajectory across all
$\calNcal \cdot \calU = \approx 14{,}160$ calibration tokens, no resets.
This is the apples-to-apples object vs.\ a batch fit.
\item[$\hat\beta_{\mathrm{MLE}}$.] \texttt{scipy.optimize.minimize\_scalar} on
the per-token NLL
$\;-\tfrac{1}{T}\sum_t \log\mathrm{softmax}(\beta\cdot\ell_t)[x_{t+1}]$
over the same calibration tokens.
\end{description}

Test evaluation: per-passage per-token NLL on the test split at four
$\beta$ candidates (T = 1, own-corpus streaming, own-corpus MLE,
\emph{other}-corpus MLE). Headline statistic is per-passage
$\Delta\log\mathrm{PPL} = \log\mathrm{PPL}(\beta) - \log\mathrm{PPL}(1)$,
with paired $t$-tests against zero across the $\calNtest$ test passages.

Three structural facts.

\paragraph{(a) The streaming filter is not online MLE.}
Calibration NLL at $\hat\beta=1$ vs at $\hat\beta_{\mathrm{MLE}}$ differs
in the fourth decimal place on both corpora
(\calNllOneWT{} vs \calNllMLEWT{} on WikiText;
\calNllOneWP{} vs \calNllMLEWP{} on WritingPrompts).
This places the Guo MLE essentially at $\log\beta=0$.  The pooled streaming
filter on the \emph{same data} settles at $\log\hat\beta =
\calLogBetaStreamPooledWT$ (WikiText) and $\calLogBetaStreamPooledWP$
(WritingPrompts) --- gaps of $\approx \calConsistencyGapWT$ and
$\calConsistencyGapWP$ nats from MLE.  The two estimators have different
fixed points.

\paragraph{(b) Why the gap.}
The filter's per-step update (\texttt{counter\_decode.py:91--182}) computes
\textbf{$q = \mathrm{softmax}(\ell_t)$}, $E_q[\ell_t]$, $\mathrm{Var}_q[\ell_t]$
--- all at $\beta = 1$, never reevaluated at the running $\hat\beta$.
This is by design: the matched-prior bootstrap-reference framework
(\texttt{bayesian-sampler/counter\_decoding.html}) defines $\ell^*_t = \ell_t /
\hat\beta$ exactly so that the score and Fisher are
$\hat\beta$-independent.  The filter is therefore a per-token Bayesian update
\emph{under that imprint model}, not an iterated Newton solver on the Guo
NLL.  Its pooled final $\hat\eta$ behaves like a single damped Newton step
from the prior mode using $\sum_t\bigl(\ell_t[x_{t+1}] - E_q[\ell_t]\bigr)$
in the numerator and $1/\sigma_0^2 + \sum_t \mathrm{Var}_q[\ell_t]$ in the
denominator --- accurate near $\beta = 1$, but increasingly biased as one
walks away from $\beta = 1$ on a non-quadratic NLL.  Figure
\ref{fig:nll-surface} shows exactly this geometry: the NLL surface is flat
near the minimum, so a small gradient at $\beta = 1$ divided by a smaller
curvature lands far away.

\paragraph{(c) Applied as a calibrator, the filter hurts test PPL.}
On WritingPrompts test, $\hat\beta_{\mathrm{stream\_med}}$ (a per-passage
estimator close to what one would actually apply online) increases per-token
PPL from \calTestPPLWPT1 to \calTestPPLWPWPstreammed{} ---
$\Delta\log\mathrm{PPL} = \calTestDeltaLogPPLWPWPstreammed$ ($t =
\calTestTStatWPWPstreammed$).  The pooled streaming $\hat\beta$ is
catastrophic.  $\hat\beta_{\mathrm{MLE}}$ is statistically indistinguishable
from $T = 1$ (paired $t = \calTestTStatWPWPMLE$, $p =
\calTestPValWPWPMLE$).  Cross-corpus MLE
(WT $\to$ WP and WP $\to$ WT) is also indistinguishable from $T=1$.

\section{What this implies for the project}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The streaming filter is not online temperature scaling.}
It measures the model's local belief about its decoder under the matched-prior
imprint hypothesis.  The Guo MLE measures the post-hoc temperature that
maximizes target-token likelihood.  On Qwen-2.5-3B the two place
$\hat\beta$ at $\log\beta = -0.94$ and $0.00$ respectively on WikiText ---
not consistent in the large-data limit.  The proposal's claim of asymptotic
equivalence (\texttt{decoding-modeling.tex}) does not survive this test and
should be removed.

\item \textbf{Qwen-2.5-3B is essentially well-calibrated} on these corpora.
The Guo NLL minimum sits at $\log\beta \approx 0$.  There is no meaningful
post-hoc temperature gain to be had from either WikiText or WritingPrompts
text in this length/model regime.  Any future Exp B (per-context sliding
$\hat\beta_t$) cannot defeat $\beta=1$ on calibration grounds alone.

\item \textbf{The filter still has an interpretable measurement target}:
the model's gradient signal at $\beta=1$, which under the imprint hypothesis
is the model's local belief about how much its prefix was temperature-scaled.
This is a different research question than calibration; the project's
framing should track that.  In particular, Exp C (entropy after a
$\beta_{\mathrm{dec}} \neq 1$ prefix) directly probes whether the model's
distribution responds to the apparent decoder of its own context.

\item \textbf{The original natural-text findings retain their narrow
content}: the per-passage filter showed no detectable baseline drift under
self-sampling (\texttt{natural\_text/findings.pdf}).  This was a
filter-pathology check, not a calibration claim, and is unaffected.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item One model (Qwen-2.5-3B), one tokenization, one passage length
($\calLength$).  The MLE-vs-streaming gap and the "Qwen is well-calibrated"
finding both depend on these.
\item No held-out validation set within the calibration half.  All
calibration-MLE fitting is on the same tokens used to compute the streaming
$\hat\beta$.  Test PPL is on disjoint passages, so the headline test result
is properly held-out, but per-corpus $\hat\beta$ comparisons are in-sample.
\item Per-passage and pooled streaming differ in aggregation; the per-passage
median is the operational quantity (what one would compute online), the
pooled trajectory is the apples-to-apples comparison vs.\ pooled MLE.  Both
disagree with MLE.  Other aggregations (mean, precision-weighted mean) were
not explored.
\end{itemize}

\section{Bottom line}

\begin{itemize}
\item Streaming filter $\neq$ Guo MLE on real data.  Different objectives,
different fixed points, large gap ($\approx 1$ nat in $\log\hat\beta$).
\item Qwen-2.5-3B is well-calibrated on these corpora; no post-hoc
temperature gain is available.
\item Applying the streaming $\hat\beta$ as a calibrator increases test PPL.
\item The filter still measures something coherent --- the model's local
belief about its decoder --- but the project's framing should treat
``calibration'' and ``imprint detection'' as distinct.
\end{itemize}
