Bootstrap, walked through

A two-token toy with explicit numbers at every step. Under matched-prior Bayes-optimality, bootstrap recovers $\ell^*_{\text{true}}$ exactly and counter-decoding holds $\beta_{\text{eff}}$ at target — visible in the arithmetic, no inference about $\beta_{\text{model}}$ as a free parameter required.

02 Step t = 0

state at start

By matched-prior Bayes-optimality, $\beta_{\text{model}}^{(0)} = \mathbb{E}_{\pi_0}[\beta] = 1.000$, and our estimator gives $\hat\beta_0 = 1.000$ — the same posterior mean from the same prior with no data.

Model output

$\ell_0 = \beta_{\text{model}}^{(0)} \cdot \ell^*_{\text{true}} = 1.000 \cdot (1, -1) = (1, -1)$.

Bootstrap recovery

$\hat\ell^*_0 = \ell_0 / \hat\beta_0 = (1, -1) / 1.000 = (1, -1) = \ell^*_{\text{true}}$✓

Counter-decode and sample

$\beta_{\text{dec}} = \beta_{\text{target}} / \hat\beta_0 = 1.5 / 1.000 = 1.500$. Sampling distribution $\text{softmax}(\beta_{\text{dec}} \cdot \ell_0) = \text{softmax}((1.5, -1.5)) = (0.953, 0.047)$.

$\beta_{\text{eff}} = \beta_{\text{dec}} \cdot \beta_{\text{model}}^{(0)} = 1.500 \cdot 1.000 = 1.500 = \beta_{\text{target}}$✓

03 Step t = 1

state at start

$\hat\beta_1 = 1.060$.

Model output

$\ell_1 = \beta_{\text{model}}^{(1)} \cdot \ell^*_{\text{true}} = 1.060 \cdot (1, -1) = (1.060, -1.060)$.

Bootstrap recovery

$\hat\ell^*_1 = \ell_1 / \hat\beta_1 = (1.060, -1.060) / 1.060 = (1, -1) = \ell^*_{\text{true}}$✓

This is the heart of the trick. The model's logits look like $(1.060, -1.060)$ — peakier than truth — because the imprint is in there. Dividing by $\hat\beta_1$ exactly removes the imprint, recovering $\ell^*_{\text{true}} = (1, -1)$. The recovery is exact, not approximate, because $\hat\beta_1 = \beta_{\text{model}}^{(1)}$ exactly (matched prior, same data).

Counter-decode and sample

$\beta_{\text{dec}} = 1.5 / 1.060 = 1.415$. Sampling distribution $\text{softmax}(1.415 \cdot (1.060, -1.060)) = \text{softmax}((1.5, -1.5)) = (0.953, 0.047)$.

$\beta_{\text{eff}} = 1.415 \cdot 1.060 = 1.500 = \beta_{\text{target}}$✓

05 The pattern

Three quantities at every step in the walkthrough:

$t$0123
$\hat\beta_t = \beta_{\text{model}}^{(t)}$1.0001.0601.1201.180
$\hat\ell^*_t$(1,-1)(1,-1)(1,-1)(1,-1)
$\beta_{\text{eff}}$1.5001.5001.500—

The middle row is the headline. $\hat\ell^*_t$ recovers $\ell^*_{\text{true}}$ exactly at every step, even though $\hat\beta_t$ itself is changing. The change in $\hat\beta_t$ — the model's accumulating in-context inference about its own temperature — is exactly the rescaling that bootstrap divides out. The bottom row is the corrector working: $\beta_{\text{eff}}$ pinned at $\beta_{\text{target}}$ at every step regardless of how $\hat\beta$ moves.

Why this works

$\beta_{\text{model}}^{(t)}$ is not a free parameter we are trying to identify from $\ell_t$. It's a deterministic function of $(\pi_0, x_{\lt t})$ — the posterior mean under the model's prior given the prefix. With matched prior, our estimator computes the same function, so $\hat\beta_t = \beta_{\text{model}}^{(t)}$ exactly. With $\hat\beta_t$ correct, bootstrap $\hat\ell^*_t = \ell_t/\hat\beta_t$ is correct. With $\hat\ell^*_t$ correct, the next likelihood factor is correct, the next posterior is correct, the next $\hat\beta$ is correct. Induction holds.

06 Scope and assumptions

Two flags on what this walkthrough does and doesn't show.

Context invariance was for narrative cleanliness, not load-bearing. The walkthrough used $\ell^*_{\text{true}} = (1, -1)$ at every step. The argument extends to context-dependent $\ell^*_{\text{true},t}(x_{\lt t})$ without modification. At step $t$, the model's logits are $\ell_t = \beta_{\text{model}}^{(t)} \cdot \ell^*_{\text{true},t}$ (now the reference is whatever the model's natural distribution function says given the actual prefix). Bootstrap gives $\hat\ell^*_t = \ell_t / \hat\beta_t = \ell^*_{\text{true},t}$ when $\hat\beta_t = \beta_{\text{model}}^{(t)}$. Same induction.

The matched prior is doing all the work. "$\hat\beta_t = \beta_{\text{model}}^{(t)}$ exactly" required that our estimator's prior $\pi_0$ matches the model's prior. If the priors differ, $\hat\beta$ drifts from $\beta_{\text{model}}$ by the calibration error, and bootstrap recovers a scaled version of $\ell^*_{\text{true}}$ rather than the truth itself, and the corrector misses target. In a real LM we don't know the model's prior over $\beta$, so this is a separate inference problem — characterize the prior empirically (probe behavior at $t=0,1$ with various interventions, see what shape of posterior best explains the model's responses), then plug in the matched prior. Worth flagging as the next experimental step after the toy phase confirms the inner loop.

What the walkthrough is not. Not a guarantee that real LMs are Bayes-optimal in this sense — that's assumption 2 of the counter-decoding memo, and it's empirically open. Not a guarantee the multiplicative imprint model is right — that's assumption 1. The walkthrough shows that conditional on those assumptions, bootstrap is sound, the corrector hits target, and the earlier non-identifiability worry was misframed. It treated $\beta_{\text{model}}$ as a free parameter to recover from $\ell_t$ alone; under Bayes-optimality $\beta_{\text{model}}$ is pinned by the prior and prefix, and the matched-prior assumption is what propagates that pinning to the estimator.
