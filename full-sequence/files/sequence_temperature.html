03 The literature

Shih, Sadigh, Ermon — "Long Horizon Temperature Scaling" (LHTS), ICML 2023 (arXiv 2302.03686). This is your realization. They name vanilla per-token tempering "myopic temperature scaling that greedily optimizes the next token," and propose sampling from temperature-scaled joint distributions instead. Their method fine-tunes a model to approximate across a range of . Honest negative result to note: LHTS did not improve MAUVE on open-ended GPT-2 generation, and both forms of tempering tended to hurt it. Read this before building anything.

Zhao et al. — "Probabilistic Inference in Language Models via Twisted SMC," ICML 2024 (arXiv 2404.17546). The inference-time alternative. They learn twist functions that estimate the expected future value of the potential at each step — i.e. a learned approximation to the above — and use it as an SMC proposal. This is the principled way to sample without fine-tuning; the twist is the object you're missing.

Gareev et al. (your own [Gareev24]) is this same local-vs-global split for truncation rather than temperature. Same disease, different operator.

Worth knowing the ancestor: this is the label-bias problem from globally- vs locally-normalized sequence models (CRFs; Andor et al. 2016). Local normalization can't represent arbitrary global distributions, and tempering locally reintroduces a label-bias-flavored pathology.

04 The nitpick: don't conflate this with the imprint

Your project doc is about the imprint (in-context Bayesian inference over decoder-induced latents). This new realization is about the local-vs-global normalization mismatch. Related, but not the same, and worth keeping distinct so you don't claim one dissolves the other.
